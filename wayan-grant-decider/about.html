About the tool

AI should make grant teams more selective.

Grant Fit Auditor turns a published call into a cited decision brief before organizations commit weeks to a proposal or reviewers commit hours to the wrong applications.

The argument

Earlier judgment beats faster volume.

Most grant technology promises faster search, faster drafting, and more submissions. That incentive is backward. A weak opportunity still consumes leadership attention, technical input, budgeting, partnerships, and staff time, even when AI produces the first draft.

Applicant mode extracts requirements, tests stated eligibility, identifies competitive gaps, and recommends whether the organization should pursue, refuse, or pursue with work.

Reviewer mode reverses the comparison. It groups applicants against the criteria the funder published, shows source text on both sides, and routes attention without producing an applicant score or funding recommendation.

The boundary

Human judgment remains visible.

  • Leaders define what the organization will pursue and what it will refuse.
  • Funders own every review disposition and every final funding decision.
  • The tool applies only criteria found in the published text.
  • The tool reads claims but does not verify whether they are accurate.
  • No database stores calls, applicant profiles, or results.

Author

Developed by Wayan Vota

Wayan Vota built Grant Fit Auditor as a working argument for social impact leaders weighing grant opportunities. He created the concept, fictional scenario, product boundaries, and editorial framing, then used OpenAI Codex to build the application.

The design choice is deliberate: make requirements inspectable, make uncertainty visible, and keep consequential decisions with people.
